Assumptions & tax sources

Full-year residents, ordinary income, standard deduction and eligible dependents with valid SSNs. Annual tax liability, not a balance due or refund after withholding.

  • Single mode covers one person. Married / separate estimates both returns; interest and medical are split by wages, and children are assigned to the husband. Community property rules are not modeled.
  • Federal: progressive 2026 brackets, child / other dependent credits (nonrefundable only), age 65+ deductions, self-employment tax, Additional Medicare and net investment income tax.
  • Traditional employee 401(k) contributions assumed. Catch-up is Roth when 2025 same-employer wages exceed $150,000. Limits exclude employer match, solo 401(k) employer contributions and plan-specific restrictions.
  • QBI: simplified eligible active sole proprietorship; 20% below the threshold, conservatively phased out above it, with the $400 active-business minimum when eligible. Wage/property tests and carryovers are omitted.
  • State: approximate 2026 brackets and basic deductions/exemptions. Separate returns use half the joint schedule. State-specific adjustments, credit phaseouts, federal-tax deductions and payroll programs are omitted.
  • Local rate is a manual estimate on wages plus positive business profit. No automatic city tax lookup.
  • Not modeled: EITC, refundable child credits, itemized deductions, AMT, capital gains, stock compensation, tips/overtime deductions or business loss limits. High-income and low-income estimates can differ materially.

Sources: IRS 2026 tax rules · IRS retirement limits · Senior deduction · Social Security · 2026 state tables

Rules reviewed November 8, 2026. State figures are a planning baseline; check your state revenue department before filing.
